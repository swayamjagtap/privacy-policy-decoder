# Privacy Policy Decoder

### Understand what you're agreeing to — without reading pages of legal jargon.

Privacy Policy Decoder turns long, difficult-to-read privacy policies into a plain-English, evidence-based privacy snapshot.

Paste a privacy policy and the application analyzes what the policy says about:

- Data collection
- Third-party sharing
- Tracking
- Data retention
- User control
- AI / model use
- Government disclosure

The system then applies a transparent, deterministic scoring rubric to produce a privacy concern grade and highlights the clauses that contributed to the result.

> **Important:** Privacy Policy Decoder provides informational analysis based on the supplied policy text. It is not legal advice and does not determine regulatory compliance.

## The Problem

Privacy policies are often long, technical, and difficult for ordinary users to interpret.

Important information can be buried across thousands of words covering:

- what data is collected,
- why it is collected,
- who it may be shared with,
- how long it may be retained,
- what choices users have,
- whether information may be used for AI or model development,
- and when information may be disclosed to authorities.

The problem isn't simply that privacy policies are long.

The bigger problem is that users often have no easy way to answer:

> **"What does this actually mean for me?"**

Existing AI assistants can summarize a policy, but summaries alone can be difficult to verify and may not provide a consistent basis for comparing results.

Privacy Policy Decoder focuses on making the analysis **structured, evidence-based, and reproducible.**

---

## The Solution

Privacy Policy Decoder separates the analysis into two stages.

### 1. AI extracts evidence

Gemini reads the supplied privacy policy and identifies evidence for seven predefined privacy dimensions.

The AI is instructed to:

- use only the supplied policy text,
- avoid legal conclusions,
- distinguish between explicit statements and missing information,
- preserve the scope and strength of the original language,
- provide concise evidence supporting each assessment.

### 2. A deterministic rubric calculates the result

The final concern score is **not generated by the AI**.

Instead, our application maps each extracted assessment to a predefined scoring rubric.

This makes the final result:

- transparent,
- repeatable,
- inspectable,
- and independent of an AI-generated overall opinion.

The application also shows the underlying evidence so users can see **why** a particular result was produced.

---

## What the Analyzer Checks

Privacy Policy Decoder evaluates seven dimensions.

| Dimension | What it looks for |
|---|---|
| **Data Collection** | How broadly the policy describes collecting personal information |
| **Third-Party Sharing** | Whether information is shared with service providers, affiliates, advertisers, or other parties |
| **Tracking** | Tracking, behavioral information, analytics, advertising-related monitoring, and similar practices |
| **Retention** | Whether the policy provides a clear or bounded retention period |
| **User Control** | Controls such as deletion, access, settings, or other user choices |
| **AI / Model Use** | Whether information or content may be used to develop, evaluate, or operate AI/model systems |
| **Government Disclosure** | Circumstances under which information may be disclosed to government or law-enforcement authorities |

---

## Transparent Scoring

Each dimension receives an assessment based on the policy text.

The application then converts those assessments into points using a fixed rubric.

### Example scoring

| Dimension | Lowest concern | Highest concern |
|---|---:|---:|
| Data Collection | 0 | 2 |
| Third-Party Sharing | 0 | 3 |
| Tracking | 0 | 2 |
| Retention | 0 | 2 |
| User Control | 0 | 2 |
| AI / Model Use | 0 | 2 |
| Government Disclosure | 0 | 2 |

Different dimensions have different weights based on the rubric.

The maximum possible score is **42 points**.

The resulting percentage is mapped to a letter grade:

| Grade | Concern label |
|---|---|
| **A** | Low Concern |
| **B** | Limited Concern |
| **C** | Moderate Concern |
| **D** | Elevated Concern |
| **E** | High Concern |
| **F** | Significant Concern |

### Why use a deterministic rubric?

The grade is deliberately separated from the AI.

The AI answers:

> **"What does the policy say?"**

The application answers:

> **"Given our published rubric, how many points does that correspond to?"**

This prevents the model from simply inventing an overall privacy grade.

---

## Evidence First

A central design principle of the project is:

> **No conclusion without supporting evidence.**

Each dimension includes the strongest relevant policy evidence identified by the analyzer.

For example, instead of simply saying:

> "The company shares data with advertisers."

the application shows the relevant policy language that led to the classification.

This gives users a way to inspect the reasoning instead of treating the result as a black box.
